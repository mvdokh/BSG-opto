**Optogenetic gating of 6–8 Hz rhythmic licking in the Licking BSG model across illumination intensities and ChR2 desensitization regimes.**

**a**, Top: Schematic of the balanced Dale-law Licking BSG network ($N = 300$: 150 E / 150 I, $C = 0.15$, $g = 1.0$, $\tau_V = 18$ ms, phase-shifted rotational connectivity $W$ with $\alpha = 0.68$, $\psi = 40^\circ$) receiving uniform global tonic drive $D \in [0, 20]$ and a 2 s 473 nm laser pulse (1–3 s) targeting a random 10% subset of excitatory neurons ($n = 15$ ChR2-expressing E cells). Bottom: Normalized ChR2 photocurrent waveforms comparing an idealized non-desensitizing step ($I_{\mathrm{ss}}/I_{\mathrm{peak}} = 1$, dashed teal), uniform fixed desensitization ($I_{\mathrm{ss}}/I_{\mathrm{peak}} = 0.3$, purple), and intensity-dependent desensitization under spatially varying illumination ($\varphi = 0.75$ and $0.30$, red; stronger illumination drives faster, deeper desensitization).

**b–d**, Representative E/I phase-sorted population firing-rate rasters (top, in sp/s; arrowheads mark the 15 illuminated E cells shaded by light factor $\varphi$) and hypoglossal (XII) protractor/retractor motor-nerve readouts (bottom, shared scale; 0.5–3.5 s window, blue bar marks 2 s laser ON, 1–3 s) under variable-light desensitizing ChR2 ($I_{\max} = 80$): **b**, at rest ($D = 0$), 10% E-cell photostimulation fails to induce rhythmic licking (unlit amplitude $\mathrm{s.d.} = 0.33$ sp/s, $\mathrm{Re}\,\lambda_{\max} = 0.48 < 1$); **c**, near the bifurcation threshold ($D = 12$, silent before laser: 0.73 sp/s), the identical 2 s laser pulse gates sustained 7.50 Hz rotational licking waves and alternating XII bursts ($\mathrm{s.d.} = 6.28$ sp/s, $\mathrm{Re}\,\lambda_{\max} = 1.04 > 1$) that cease cleanly after laser offset (post: 1.65 sp/s); **d**, reference spontaneous licking driven solely by suprathreshold tonic drive ($D = 20$, no laser: $\mathrm{s.d.} = 12.92$ sp/s, $f = 7.50$ Hz).

**e**, Complete rest-state ($D = 0$) phase space across peak photocurrent $I_{\max} = 10\text{–}320$ and ChR2 steady-state desensitization ratio $I_{\mathrm{ss}}/I_{\mathrm{peak}} = 0.1\text{–}1.0$ plus spatially varying illumination (`var. φ`; $n = 30$ random 15-cell subsets per cell). Across all 36 regimes, 10% E-cell photostimulation fails to trigger licking (maximum mean unlit amplitude $\mathrm{s.d.} = 0.64$ sp/s; maximum mean $\mathrm{Re}\,\lambda_{\max} = 0.79 < 1$).

**f, g**, Mean unlit oscillation amplitude (rate s.d., sp/s; shared colour scale with **e**, $n = 20$ subsets per cell) across background tonic drive $D = 0\text{–}20$ and peak photocurrent $I_{\max}$ (`Off` and `5–320`) for **f**, realistic desensitizing ChR2 with variable illumination (`var. φ`), and **g**, idealized non-desensitizing step current ($I_{\mathrm{ss}}/I_{\mathrm{peak}} = 1$). Dotted cyan line marks the spontaneous no-laser bifurcation threshold ($D_{\mathrm{th}} \approx 13.5$); dashed white contour marks $\mathrm{s.d.} = 2$ sp/s. Removing desensitization lowers the gating drive window from $D \approx 10\text{–}13$ down to $D \approx 8\text{–}13$, while excessive non-desensitizing photocurrent ($I_{\max} \ge 160$) induces saturation suppression by pushing neurons onto the flat saturation region of the firing-rate transfer function.

**h**, Mean unlit oscillation amplitude (rate s.d., sp/s; $n = 30$ random 15-cell subsets) across $D = 0\text{–}20$ comparing no laser (black), variable-light desensitizing ChR2 ($I_{\max} = 40$ and $80$, red), uniform desensitizing ChR2 ($I_{\max} = 40$, purple), and non-desensitizing step ($I_{\max} = 40$, dashed teal). At $D = 12$, realistic desensitizing illumination raises amplitude from 0.76 sp/s (no laser) to 3.44 sp/s ($I_{\max} = 40$) and 6.62 sp/s ($I_{\max} = 80$), whereas rest ($D = 0$) remains silent (0.28–0.54 sp/s across all conditions).

**i**, Leading Jacobian real eigenvalue $\mathrm{Re}\,\lambda_{\max}(J)$ across $D = 0\text{–}20$ ($n = 12$ subsets per curve) explaining the state-dependent gating mechanism: at low tonic drive, neurons sit on the shallow subthreshold tail of the transfer function so 10% E-cell photostimulation cannot push $\mathrm{Re}\,\lambda_{\max}$ across the Hopf instability boundary ($\mathrm{Re}\,\lambda_{\max} = 1$, dashed line); near $D_{\mathrm{th}}$, the same photocurrent shifts the population onto steeper gain and crosses unity.

**j**, Peak oscillation frequency (Hz) of gated rhythms across $D = 10\text{–}20$ (plotted where mean unlit $\mathrm{s.d.} \ge 2$ sp/s; green shading marks the 6–8 Hz physiological licking band). Optogenetically gated licking remains locked within the physiological 6–8 Hz range across illumination intensities and desensitization conditions (e.g., 7.52 $\pm$ 0.04 Hz at $D = 12$, $I_{\max} = 80$, vs. 7.50 $\pm$ 0.00 Hz spontaneous at $D = 20$), showing that near-threshold photostimulation gates the intrinsic brainstem rhythm without distorting its frequency.
