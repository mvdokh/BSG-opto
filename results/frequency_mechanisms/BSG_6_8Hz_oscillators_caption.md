**Comparison of alternative circuit architectures for generating a 6–8 Hz balanced Dale-law oscillator.**

**a**, Representative 1 s steady-state phase-sorted E and I firing-rate rasters (sp/s; ordered by increasing activation time so traveling waves propagate top-left to bottom-right) for the four circuit architectures achieving 30/30 networks inside 6–8 Hz at $D = 20$: asymmetric E/I time constants ($\tau_E = 12$ ms, $\tau_I = 55$ ms, purely random $W$), discrete 3-cluster cyclic Dale wiring ($\tau_V = 18$ ms, exact $W\mathbf{1} = 0$), steep rotational Dale $W$ at original slow $\tau_V = 50$ ms ($\psi = 68^\circ, N = 500, C = 0.20, W_{\mathrm{bulk}} = 0.55, \alpha = 0.85$), and the Licking BSG ($\psi = 40^\circ, N = 300, C = 0.15, W_{\mathrm{bulk}} = 0.58, \alpha = 0.68, \tau_V = 18$ ms).

**b**, Polar histograms of neuronal oscillation phases at the peak frequency for E (orange) and I (blue) populations (centered on mean E phase $= 0^\circ$), with normalized 12-bin phase entropy $H \in [0, 1]$ reported below each rose plot. Asymmetric $\tau_E/\tau_I$ collapses all E and I neurons into narrow synchronous phase spikes ($H = 0.28$); 3-cluster cyclic wiring concentrates phases into three discrete $120^\circ$-spaced lobes ($H = 0.88$); both steep rotational $W$ and the Licking BSG tile all $360^\circ$ uniformly ($H = 1.00$ and $1.00$).

**c**, Normalized population power spectral density (0–14 Hz) for the four architectures in **a**, each exhibiting a sharp narrowband spectral peak inside the grey shaded 6–8 Hz target band.

**d**, Peak oscillation frequency across $n = 30$ random seeds (translucent dots with mean $\pm$ s.d. overlaid; fraction of all 30 seeds landing inside 6–8 Hz annotated at top) across all eight tested mechanisms. Random $W$ at $\tau_V = 18$ ms (1.25 $\pm$ 0.71 Hz, 0/30), rate adaptation ($\beta = 0.4, \tau_a = 80$ ms at $D = 35$; 4.04 $\pm$ 1.25 Hz, 2/30), and ultra-fast $\tau_V = 4$ ms (5.59 $\pm$ 3.31 Hz, 4/30) fail to lock reliably inside 6–8 Hz, whereas delayed inhibition ($d_I = 35$ ms, 6.88 $\pm$ 0.00 Hz), asymmetric $\tau_E/\tau_I$ (7.19 $\pm$ 0.00 Hz), 3-cluster cyclic Dale (7.17 $\pm$ 0.36 Hz), steep rotational $W$ (7.44 $\pm$ 0.13 Hz), and the Licking BSG (7.30 $\pm$ 0.31 Hz) all lock 30/30 seeds inside 6–8 Hz.

**e**, Trade-off between target-band reliability (% of networks inside 6–8 Hz) and sequence structure (phase entropy $H$). Purely random-$W$ routes that lock 100% into 6–8 Hz (asymmetric $\tau_E/\tau_I$ and delayed inhibition) sacrifice sequence generation ($H = 0.28$ and $0.00$), whereas rotational Dale architectures occupy the upper-right quadrant (100% target reliability and high phase diversity), with the Licking BSG achieving continuous rotational waves using physiological $\tau_V = 18$ ms and modest wiring bias ($\alpha = 0.68$).
