**Testing purely random balanced Dale connectivity ($W$) without rotational wiring ($\alpha = 0$, no phase shift $\psi$) at $N = 300$, $C = 0.15$, and fast $\tau_V$.**

**a**, Oscillation amplitude (temporal firing-rate s.d., sp/s) versus peak oscillation frequency (Hz) at $D = 20$ across $n = 200$ independent purely random balanced Dale networks with $N = 300$ (150 E / 150 I), $C = 0.15$, $g = 1.0$, $W_{\mathrm{radius}} = 1.0$, and $\tau_V = 18$ ms (red: complex leading eigenvalue $\mathrm{Im}\,\lambda_1 \neq 0$, $n = 122$; blue: purely real $\mathrm{Im}\,\lambda_1 = 0$, $n = 78$; green band: 6–8 Hz physiological licking target). Although 155/200 (77.5%) networks oscillate above 2 sp/s, their median frequency is only 1.25 Hz (maximum 4.25 Hz) and **0/200 (0.0%)** reach 6–8 Hz.

**b**, Phase-sorted firing-rate raster (sp/s) and antagonist motor-nerve readout (2–5 s steady state at $D = 20$) for the single fastest strongly oscillating purely random network out of all 200 seeds at $\tau_V = 18$ ms (`seed = 80`, $\lambda_1 = 0.96 \pm 0.13i$, amplitude s.d. $= 10.35$ sp/s, $f = 3.50$ Hz), confirming that $\tau_V = 18$ ms without structured connectivity remains below 6–8 Hz.

**c**, Peak oscillation frequency at $D = 20$ across $n = 100$ purely random networks ($N = 300$, $C = 0.15$, $dt = 0.25$ ms, membrane voltage noise variance held constant at $\sigma_V = 0.40$ across all $\tau_V$ matching original BSG at $\tau_V = 50$ ms) as $\tau_V$ is reduced from 50 ms down to 3 ms. Dots show individual rhythmic networks ($\mathrm{s.d.} \ge 2$ sp/s); solid black line tracks the median frequency. Purely random $W$ can reach the 6–8 Hz band without rotational wiring if $\tau_V$ is lowered to **~3–5 ms** (median 6.67 Hz at $\tau_V = 4$ ms, matching fast AMPA/GABA$_A$ brainstem synaptic timescales), but individual random realizations scatter widely across frequency (from 0.3 to 15.7 Hz at $\tau_V = 4$ ms).

**d**, Percentage of purely random networks that oscillate (rate $\mathrm{s.d.} \ge 2$ sp/s, black) and percentage that land inside the 6–8 Hz licking band (red) as a function of $\tau_V$ (matched membrane noise $\sigma_V = 0.40$). Reducing $\tau_V$ alone never removes real-eigenvalue fixed-point failures (oscillation rate remains ~75% across all $\tau_V$), and because random $|\mathrm{Im}\,\lambda_1|$ varies widely across seeds, the fraction landing inside 6–8 Hz peaks at only **17%** (at $\tau_V = 4$ ms), compared to 100% with rotational connectivity.
