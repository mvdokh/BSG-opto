**Fig. 2 | Design, parameter ablation, and 200-network validation of a 6–8 Hz mammalian licking BSG model.**

**a**, Systematic parameter ablation ($n = 30$ random realizations per condition at $D = 20$; bars show median peak frequency among rhythmic models, error bars show interquartile range, percentages denote fraction with temporal rate $\mathrm{s.d.} \ge 2$ sp/s; green band marks the 6–8 Hz rodent licking frequency range). Scaling neuron count ($N = 500$), connection probability ($C = 0.25$), or transfer gain ($2.0$) individually preserves slow oscillations ($< 1$ Hz). Reducing the effective membrane/synaptic time constant $\tau_V$ alone from 50 ms to 18 ms yields only modest speedup (1.00 Hz) while retaining fixed-point failures (70% rhythmic); introducing phase-shifted rotational Dale connectivity alone ($\alpha = 0.68$, $\psi = 40^\circ$) lifts reliability to 100% and multiplies frequency (2.75 Hz). Combining mammalian $\tau_V = 18$ ms with rotational Dale connectivity ($N = 300$, $C = 0.15$) places median frequency directly in the physiological licking band (7.25 Hz, 100% rhythmic).

**b**, Complex eigenvalue spectrum of the Licking BSG weight matrix (`seed = 1`, red; $N = 300$, 150 E / 150 I, $C = 0.15$, $g = 1$, nominal bulk radius $W_{\mathrm{bulk}} = 0.58$, $\alpha = 0.68$, $\psi = 40^\circ$) overlaid on the original BSG spectrum (grey; nominal radius $W_{\mathrm{radius}} = 1.0$). Strict Dale's law and exact per-neuron E/I in-degree balance ($W\mathbf{1} = 0$) are preserved while isolating a fast rotational complex-conjugate eigenmode ($\lambda_1 = 0.96 \pm 0.83i$) cleanly outside the random bulk. Inset: phase-shifted excitatory ($p_E \propto 1 + \alpha\cos(\Delta\theta - \psi)$, red) and anti-phase inhibitory ($p_I \propto 1 - \alpha\cos(\Delta\theta - \psi)$, blue) connection-probability kernels.

**c**, Representative 6–8 Hz licking bout (`seed = 1`, global drive $D = 20$ turned on at $t = 1.0$ s) showing phase-sorted E and I firing-rate rasters (top, in sp/s) and simulated antagonist hypoglossal (XII) protractor/retractor nerve readouts (bottom; ~138 ms cycle period, ~7.3 Hz).

**d**, Normalized population power spectral density comparing the Licking BSG model (narrowband peak at ~7.25 Hz inside the 6–8 Hz band) against the original BSG model (peak at 0.75 Hz). Inset: smooth circular rotational trajectory in PC1–PC2 state space.

**e**, Population oscillation amplitude (mean temporal firing-rate s.d., sp/s) across tonic drive $D = 1\text{–}20$ for $n = 200$ random Licking BSG realizations (median, IQR, and 5th–95th percentile bands in red; 100.0% rhythmic at $D = 20$, median $\mathrm{s.d.} = 15.26$ sp/s, median onset threshold $D_{\mathrm{th}} = 13$) compared against the 1,000-model original BSG median (dashed grey).

**f**, Peak oscillation frequency distribution at $D = 20$ across all $n = 200$ Licking BSG networks (red; median 7.25 Hz, 5th–95th percentiles 6.75–7.75 Hz; 100.0% inside 6–8 Hz) versus rhythmic original BSG realizations (grey; median 0.50 Hz).

**g**, Independent control of licking frequency versus amplitude (mean $\pm$ s.d., $n = 10$ Licking networks at $D = 20$): selective gain modulation of the top/bottom 15% frequency-sensitive neurons smoothly steers licking frequency across the physiological 6–8 Hz range (from 6.47 $\pm$ 0.28 Hz at modulation index $-0.5$ to 8.03 $\pm$ 0.38 Hz at $+0.5$, red) at nearly constant amplitude, whereas uniform gain modulation across all neurons ($1.05\text{–}1.50$) holds frequency invariant at 7.28 Hz (dash-dotted grey) while scaling oscillation amplitude 2.4-fold (8.9 to 21.6 sp/s). Because the model runs natively at $\mathrm{d}t = 1$ ms with preserved threshold $D_{\mathrm{th}} \approx 13$, it remains drop-in compatible with `BSG-opto` (10% E-cell photostimulation at $I_{\max} = 40$ yields 0.28 $\pm$ 0.00 sp/s at $D = 0$ vs. 3.63 $\pm$ 1.56 sp/s at $D = 12$).
