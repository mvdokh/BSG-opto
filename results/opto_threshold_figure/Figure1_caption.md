Fig. 1 | Sparse optogenetic excitation fails to initiate rhythmic motor activity unless the balanced spinal network is near threshold, where subset-to-subset variability is governed by multi-synaptic recurrent amplification.

a, Top: balanced spinal motor network model (Lindén et al., 2022; N = 200 neurons: 100 excitatory [E], 100 inhibitory [I]; pairwise connection probability C = 0.1; inhibitory-to-excitatory weight ratio g = 1; spectral radius = 1.0) receiving uniform global tonic drive D (0–20) to all neurons and a 5 s photostimulation pulse (473 nm laser, 1–6 s) targeted to a random 10% of E cells (n = 10). Bottom: normalized channelrhodopsin-2 (ChR2) photocurrents (I/I_max) comparing a non-desensitizing step (I_ss/I_peak = 1.0, dashed teal), uniform desensitizing ChR2 (I_ss/I_peak = 0.3, tau_des = 40 ms, tau_off = 15 ms, violet), and spatially heterogeneous illumination (per-cell phi ~ U(0, 1)) with intensity-dependent desensitization (I_ss/I_peak scaling from 0.9 to 0.3, tau_des from 150 to 40 ms, half-saturation K = 0.25; shown for phi = 0.75 and 0.30, crimson).

b–d, Representative phase-sorted population firing-rate rasters (top; E and I separated by horizontal white line) and antagonist flexor/extensor motor-nerve readouts (bottom; opposite-phase pools at +/-pi/2, spread pi/8). Red arrowheads indicate the 10 opsin E cells shaded by phi; blue bars and dashed lines denote the 1–6 s laser epoch (variable light, I_max = 80). b, From rest (D = 0), laser stimulation drives tonic firing in the illuminated E cells but fails to induce rotational population waves or rhythmic nerve bursts (unlit temporal s.d. = 0.21 Hz, leading Jacobian eigenvalue Re lambda_max = 0.53). c, With subthreshold background tonic drive near the intrinsic bifurcation (D = 12; pre-laser s.d. = 0.52 Hz, post-laser s.d. = 1.14 Hz), the same 10% E photostimulation gates full-population rotational firing and alternating nerve bursts during light delivery (s.d. = 5.35 Hz, Re lambda_max = 1.05). d, Suprathreshold global drive (D = 20, no laser) generates sustained intrinsic rhythmicity throughout the trial (s.d. = 8.60 Hz, Re lambda_max = 1.12).

e, Unlit rhythmicity (mean temporal s.d. across the 190 unlit neurons during 2–6 s; n = 30 random 10% E draws per tile) at rest (D = 0) across peak photocurrent intensities (I_max = 10–320) and ChR2 desensitization regimes (uniform I_ss/I_peak = 0.1–1.0 and variable-light var. phi). No combination evokes rhythmicity (all 36 tiles <= 0.43 Hz vs 0.15 Hz without light and 8.69 +/- 0.27 Hz at D = 20; max Re lambda_max = 0.79 < 1). Illuminating a random 10% of all neurons (20/200 mixed E/I cells, I_max = 80) at D = 0 similarly fails (0.20 +/- 0.05 Hz desensitizing; 0.24 +/- 0.07 Hz step).

f, g, Phase diagrams of unlit rhythmicity across background tonic drive D (0–20) and peak intensity I_max (5–320; leftmost column 'Off' shows matched no-laser controls, n = 30; laser tiles, n = 20) for intensity-dependent desensitizing ChR2 (f) and non-desensitizing step current (g), on the shared colour scale of e. Dotted cyan lines indicate the intrinsic bifurcation threshold without light (D_th approx. 13.5); dashed white contours mark rhythmicity onset (s.d. = 2.0 Hz). Photostimulation fails for all D <= 8 regardless of intensity or desensitization, gates rhythms only near D_th (D approx. 10–13) at moderate effective plateau currents, and suppresses intrinsic suprathreshold rhythms at excessive sustained currents (I_max >= 160 in f; I_max >= 40 in g) by pinning opsin cells near saturation.

h, i, Unlit rhythmicity (h) and linearized Re lambda_max of diag(f'(V_bar))W (i) versus background tonic drive D (mean +/- s.d.; n = 30 draws in h, n = 15 in i) at I_max = 40 across ChR2 regimes versus no laser. Pink shading highlights the near-threshold window (D = 10–13.5). For D <= 10, 0% of draws evoke rhythms (s.d. >= 2 Hz and >= 2x baseline) and Re lambda_max < 1. Near threshold, variable-light desensitizing stimulation lifts Re lambda_max to or above 1 and evokes rhythms in 23% (D = 11), 50% (D = 12; 2.22 +/- 1.28 vs 0.72 +/- 0.05 Hz no laser), and 60% (D = 13; 3.01 +/- 1.64 vs 1.20 +/- 0.16 Hz) of draws, whereas non-desensitizing step current at I_max = 40 saturates opsin cells and caps Re lambda_max <= 0.93. Results replicate across n = 10 independent random network realizations (15 draws each, I_max = 40: D = 0 laser 0.17 +/- 0.00 vs 0.15 +/- 0.00 Hz no laser; D = 12 laser 1.92 +/- 0.48 vs 0.89 +/- 0.26 Hz; D = 20 reference 7.27 +/- 3.02 Hz).

j, Complex eigenvalue spectra of diag(f'(V_bar))W during 2–6 s for the representative draw in b–d (open rings mark leading conjugate pairs). At D = 0, sparse E stimulation leaves unlit E and I neurons on the low-gain foot of the sigmoidal transfer function f(V), trapping the entire spectrum inside Re lambda < 1 (dashed line); only when background drive already places the network near threshold does 10% E stimulation push the oscillatory conjugate pair across Re lambda = 1.

k, Repeated random shuffling of which 10 excitatory cells receive illumination (n = 500 independent subsets per condition; horizontal black ticks show median, vertical bars interquartile range; percentages denote draws with s.d. >= 2 Hz). At rest (D = 0), shuffling yields virtually zero variability and universal failure (variable light I_max = 40: 0.17 +/- 0.01 Hz, range 0.16–0.20 Hz, 0/500 >= 2 Hz, max Re lambda_max = 0.68; uniform I_max = 80: 0.34 +/- 0.15 Hz, median 0.31 Hz, 1/500 >= 2 Hz). Near threshold (D = 12, I_max = 40), shuffling produces nearly 20-fold variability (variable light: 2.06 +/- 1.51 Hz, range 0.49–8.41 Hz, 36.2% >= 2 Hz; uniform light: 2.27 +/- 1.78 Hz, range 0.49–9.55 Hz, 40.4% >= 2 Hz). Variance decomposition across 20 fixed 10-cell subsets repeated over 25 independent noise realizations each (D = 12, uniform I_max = 40) confirms that 99.4% of total variance reflects deterministic cell-subset identity (subset means 0.98–10.05 Hz) and only 0.6% reflects trial-to-trial membrane noise (mean within-subset s.d. = 0.17 Hz).

l, Analytical zero-simulation prediction of the 500-shuffle rhythmicity outcomes at D = 12 (variable light, I_max = 40) from the connectivity matrix W alone. The steady-state linear-response voltage shift V_lin = D + (I - f'(D)W)^(-1) I_ss is used to evaluate the predicted leading eigenvalue Re lambda_max^pred of diag(f'(V_lin))W (x-axis) and the net multi-synaptic unlit depolarization <Delta V> (colour scale). Predicted Re lambda_max^pred tightly tracks simulated rhythmicity (Spearman rho = 0.84, Pearson r = 0.77, n = 500), exhibiting a sharp transition across Re lambda_max^pred = 1. Individual E neurons span net inhibitory to strongly excitatory multi-synaptic population impact (g_eff = -0.070 to +0.126 per unit current); causally selecting the top-10 versus bottom-10 E cells by analytical g_eff alone (red and blue diamonds, uniform I_max = 40) produces an almost 10-fold separation at D = 12 (3.76 Hz, Re lambda_max = 1.02 vs 0.39 Hz, Re lambda_max = 0.75) while still failing to evoke rhythms at D = 0 (0.43 Hz, Re lambda_max = 0.88).

m, Variance in 500-shuffle D = 12 rhythmicity explained (R^2; |Spearman rho| in parentheses) by candidate predictors. Multi-synaptic linear-response stability (Re lambda_max^pred: R^2 = 0.59, rho = 0.84; uniform-light shuffles: R^2 = 0.54, rho = 0.80) and net multi-synaptic depolarization (<Delta V>: R^2 = 0.48, rho = 0.75) account for most subset-to-subset variability, whereas direct 1-hop E-minus-I out-degree (R^2 = 0.08, rho = 0.31), total steady photocurrent (R^2 = 0.01), dominant eigenmode amplitude |v_i| (R^2 < 0.001), and oscillatory phase coherence R (R^2 < 0.001) have little or no explanatory power.
