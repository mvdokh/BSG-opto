**Oscillation amplitude versus oscillation frequency at $D = 20$ across 1,000 random BSG networks.**

Joint scatter plot and marginal distributions of steady-state oscillation amplitude (mean temporal firing-rate s.d. across all $N = 200$ neurons, in sp/s) versus peak oscillation frequency (FFT spectral peak over the 6 s steady-state window, in Hz; small horizontal jitter $\pm 0.04$ Hz added strictly within each 0.167 Hz FFT frequency bin for visibility) for all $n = 1{,}000$ independent random balanced Dale networks ($N = 200$, $C = 0.1$, $g = 1.0$, $W_{\mathrm{radius}} = 1.0$, $\tau_V = 50$ ms) driven at global tonic drive $D = 20$. Red points and histogram bars denote networks with a complex leading eigenvalue ($\mathrm{Im}\,\lambda_1 \neq 0$, rotational dominant mode; $n = 548$); blue points and bars denote networks with a purely real leading eigenvalue ($\mathrm{Im}\,\lambda_1 = 0$, non-rotational dominant mode; $n = 452$); amber star marks the original reference network (`seed = 1`, 0.67 Hz, 8.81 sp/s); horizontal dashed line marks the 2 sp/s rhythmicity threshold (735/1,000 rhythmic, 73.5%). Among rhythmic networks, oscillation amplitude and oscillation frequency are uncorrelated (Spearman $\rho = 0.03$, $p = 0.44$), and all 1,000 random realizations remain strictly below 1.7 Hz (median rhythmic frequency 0.50 Hz, maximum 1.67 Hz).
