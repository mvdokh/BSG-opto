**Fig. 1 | Characterization of oscillation amplitude, fixed-point failures, and oscillation frequency across 1,000 random realizations of the original BSG model.**

**a**, Population oscillation amplitude—quantified as the mean temporal standard deviation (s.d.) of neuronal firing rate in spikes per second (sp/s) across all $N = 200$ neurons over a 6 s steady-state window after 2 s burn-in—as a function of uniform global tonic drive $D = 1\text{–}20$ across $n = 1{,}000$ independent random balanced Dale networks ($N = 200$, 100 E / 100 I, $C = 0.1$, $g = 1.0$, nominal spectral radius $W_{\mathrm{radius}} = 1.0$, $\tau_V = 50$ ms, transfer-function midpoint $V_{\mathrm{th}} = 20$, gain $= 1.2$, $f_{\max} = 50$ sp/s above midpoint, noise $= 4.0$). Note that temporal rate s.d. (sp/s) measures how strongly neuronal firing rates swing up and down over time (oscillation amplitude), whereas oscillation frequency (cycles per second, Hz) is shown separately in **c** and **f**. Solid black curve with markers shows the overall median across all 1,000 networks (reaching 7.24 sp/s at $D = 20$); solid red and solid blue curves show the medians of networks with complex leading eigenvalues ($\mathrm{Im}\,\lambda_1 \neq 0$, rotational dominant mode; $n = 548$, 54.8%) versus purely real leading eigenvalues ($\mathrm{Im}\,\lambda_1 = 0$, non-rotational dominant mode; $n = 452$, 45.2%); dashed amber curve marks the original reference network (`seed = 1`); horizontal dashed grey line marks the 2 sp/s rhythmicity threshold.

**b**, Percentage of random networks exhibiting rhythmic firing (temporal rate $\mathrm{s.d.} \ge 2$ sp/s, solid black; strong rhythm $\mathrm{s.d.} \ge 5$ sp/s, dashed grey) across $D = 1\text{–}20$. At $D = 20$, 73.5% (735/1,000) of all networks oscillate above 2 sp/s and 60.0% (600/1,000) exceed 5 sp/s: 93.4% of complex-$\lambda_1$ ($\mathrm{Im}\,\lambda_1 \neq 0$, rotational) networks oscillate (red) versus only 49.3% of real-$\lambda_1$ ($\mathrm{Im}\,\lambda_1 = 0$, non-rotational) networks (blue). Inset: distribution of onset tonic drive $D_{\mathrm{th}}$ (first drive with temporal rate $\mathrm{s.d.} \ge 2$ sp/s, $n = 942$ networks; median $D_{\mathrm{th}} = 14$).

**c**, Representative phase-sorted population firing-rate rasters (top, in sp/s) and antagonist flexor/extensor motor-nerve readouts (bottom; shared scale, 2–8 s steady state) at $D = 20$ illustrating three dynamical archetypes, with coloured dots following each title indicating membership in the groups from **a** and **b** (red: complex $\lambda_1$; grey: strong rhythm $\mathrm{s.d.} \ge 5$ sp/s; amber: reference `seed = 1`; blue: real $\lambda_1$): the classic reference oscillator (`seed = 1`, $\lambda_1 = 0.98 \pm 0.20i$, amplitude $\mathrm{s.d.} = 8.33$ sp/s, oscillation frequency $f = 0.67$ Hz), a faster complex-mode oscillator (`seed = 868`, $\lambda_1 = 0.92 \pm 0.48i$, amplitude $\mathrm{s.d.} = 8.13$ sp/s, $f = 1.67$ Hz), and a purely real leading-eigenvalue realization trapped in a static non-oscillatory fixed point (`seed = 631`, $\lambda_1 = 1.10$, amplitude $\mathrm{s.d.} = 0.56$ sp/s).

**d**, Leading connectivity eigenvalue $(\mathrm{Re}\,\lambda_1, |\mathrm{Im}\,\lambda_1|)$ of each of the 1,000 random matrices $W$, coloured by temporal rate s.d. (sp/s) at $D = 20$. Networks whose rightmost eigenvalue lands on the real axis ($\mathrm{Im}\,\lambda_1 = 0$) predominantly undergo a non-oscillatory pitchfork-like instability rather than a Hopf oscillation.

**e**, Temporal firing-rate s.d. (oscillation amplitude, sp/s) versus spatial s.d. of time-averaged neuronal rates (sp/s) at $D = 20$, separating true rhythmic oscillators (high temporal s.d.) from static heterogeneous fixed-point states (low temporal s.d. $< 2$ sp/s but elevated spatial rate s.d., median 10.85 sp/s among real-$\lambda_1$ networks).

**f**, Distribution of simulated peak oscillation frequencies (cycles per second, Hz; 0.167 Hz FFT bin width) at $D = 20$ across all rhythmic BSG networks ($n = 735$, temporal rate $\mathrm{s.d.} \ge 2$ sp/s); vertical dashed line marks the median. Because $\tau_V = 50$ ms and random Dale matrices yield modest $|\mathrm{Im}\,\lambda_1|$, the original BSG model oscillates strictly in a slow regime (median 0.50 Hz, mean 0.51 $\pm$ 0.31 Hz, maximum 1.67 Hz across all 1,000 models), never approaching fast mammalian licking frequencies (6–8 Hz).
